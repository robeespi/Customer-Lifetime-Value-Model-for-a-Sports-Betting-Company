# Customer-Lifetime-Value-Model-for-a-Sports-Betting-Company
Developed a Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) modelling solution for a sports betting company to identify customers with the highest long-term value potential. I analysed betting behaviour, product mix, stake types and customer characteristics, performed data-quality validation and exploratory analysis, and designed a profitability-focused ML approach. The model enables customer segmentation and supports targeted marketing, promotions, retention and acquisition strategies.

#Project Overview

This project explores the development of a Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) modelling framework for a sports betting company, with the objective of identifying customers who are expected to generate the greatest long-term net profit.

Rather than focusing solely on customer spending or betting turnover, the approach frames CLV around profitability over the customer's lifecycle. This allows the business to distinguish between customers who generate high revenue and customers who are genuinely valuable after considering factors such as payouts, bonuses, betting behaviour and other variable costs.

The project combines data quality assessment, exploratory data analysis, feature engineering, predictive modelling and customer segmentation to translate betting data into actionable commercial insights.

#Business Problem

Sports betting businesses need to understand not only how much customers bet, but which customers are likely to remain profitable over time.

A customer with high betting activity is not necessarily a high-value customer. For example, customers who frequently use bonuses, consistently generate losses for the bookmaker, or exploit pricing opportunities may generate significant turnover while providing limited or negative profitability.

The proposed CLV framework therefore focuses on:

Predicting the future net profit generated by a customer based on their early betting behaviour and customer characteristics.

The objective is to use this information to support:

Customer segmentation
Targeted marketing
Promotional allocation
Retention strategies
Acquisition decisions
Risk management
Product and pricing strategies

The project recommends measuring CLV as total expected net profit from a customer over their lifecycle, rather than simply revenue or betting turnover.

#Data Quality Assessment

The first stage of the project involved inspecting the betting dataset for inconsistencies and invalid records.

A total of 864 rows were identified with data-quality issues, including:

Invalid betting amounts
Logical inconsistencies in return calculations
Invalid betting prices
Invalid payout calculations
Combinations of multiple data-quality issues

The largest issue was related to invalid betting amounts, with 830 records containing betting amounts less than or equal to zero.

This stage highlights the importance of validating transactional data before using it for customer-level modelling.

#Exploratory Data Analysis

The exploratory analysis investigated profitability across several dimensions of betting behaviour.

Racing vs Sports

The analysis identified a significant difference between product categories:

Racing bets were profitable
Sports bets were loss-making

This raised potential business questions around pricing, risk management, customer composition and potential sharp-bettor or bonus-abuse behaviour.

Price / Odds Analysis

Profitability also varied across betting price ranges.

The analysis found that the extreme ends of the price spectrum tended to produce stronger margins, while mid-range prices between approximately 2 and 20 showed comparatively lower margins.

This provides a useful perspective for understanding the relationship between customer betting behaviour, probability of winning and bookmaker profitability.

Cash vs Bonus Bets

Stake type was another important driver.

The analysis showed that:

Cash bets were solidly profitable.
Bonus bets were highly unprofitable.

This suggests that bonus-dependent behaviour should be incorporated into customer value modelling and potentially into promotional targeting strategies.

Customer-Level Profitability

The data was aggregated to the customer level to examine the relationship between average betting amount and total return to the bookmaker.

Most customers were concentrated around relatively small average betting amounts, while higher-value customers showed considerably greater variability in returns. The analysis also identified customers generating significant negative returns for the bookmaker, highlighting the importance of distinguishing high turnover from high profitability.

#CLV Modelling Approach

The proposed modelling framework uses a customer's first 20 bets to predict their future profitability.

The target variable is defined as:

Customer Lifetime Value = Total Net Profit Generated by a Customer Over Their Lifecycle

Future gross profit can incorporate:

Revenue from bets − payouts − variable costs

where variable costs may include:

Bonuses
Marketing/acquisition costs
Transaction costs

This approach aligns the model with the commercial objective of profit maximisation rather than simply maximising betting turnover.

#Feature Engineering

Features were designed around several dimensions of customer behaviour.

1. Betting Behaviour

Features include:

Average bet size
Standard deviation of bet size
Betting frequency
Number of active days
Early betting outcomes
Expected value of early bets
Margin per bet
Cash vs bonus stake mix

2. Product Mix & Risk Appetite

Additional features capture:

Racing vs sports betting mix
Long-shot betting behaviour
Multi vs single bets
Percentage of bets using bonuses
3. Customer Profile & Acquisition

Where available, the framework incorporates:

Acquisition channel
Signup bonus usage
Estimated affluence
Location
Device type
Signup timing
4. Behavioural Trends

The model can also capture:

Time between first and 20th bet
Recency of activity
Responsiveness to promotions
Conversion from bonus activity to cash activity

These features are intended to capture not just how much a customer bets, but how the customer behaves and how that behaviour relates to future profitability.

# Model Development & Evaluation

Several modelling approaches were considered.

Baseline Model

A Linear Regression model can be used as an interpretable baseline and to establish whether there is a meaningful linear relationship between customer characteristics and future CLV.

Machine Learning

XGBoost was identified as a suitable approach for modelling nonlinear relationships and interactions within tabular customer data.

Alternative Approaches

Depending on the availability and structure of future customer data, Bayesian and survival modelling approaches could also be used to jointly model customer value and churn behaviour.

# Evaluation Strategy

A key consideration is preventing information leakage between customers and across time.

The recommended approach is therefore to use:

Customer-level train/validation/test splits
Temporal validation
Earlier customer cohorts for training
Later cohorts for out-of-sample testing

Model evaluation should combine traditional predictive metrics with business-oriented metrics, including:

MAE
RMSE
Profit Recall
Top-K customer profitability
ROI simulation
A/B testing of targeted campaigns

This ensures that the model is evaluated not only on statistical accuracy but also on its ability to identify customers who actually generate future business value.

# Model Interpretability

For a business-facing ML solution, understanding why a customer receives a particular CLV prediction is as important as the prediction itself.

The framework therefore proposes using:

Feature importance
SHAP values
Customer-level explanations

These techniques can help marketing and commercial teams understand which behaviours are associated with higher or lower predicted profitability.

# From Predictions to Business Decisions

The ultimate objective is not simply to generate a CLV score.

The model can be used to segment customers into:

🟢 High CLV

Customers predicted to have strong long-term profitability.

Potential actions:

Loyalty programs
VIP experiences
Personalised engagement
Retention initiatives
🟡 Medium CLV

Customers with potential to become more valuable.

Potential actions:

Targeted offers
Personalised promotions
Engagement campaigns
Cross-sell opportunities
🔴 Low CLV / Bonus-Dependent

Customers with limited profitability or potentially negative economics.

Potential actions:

Reduce unnecessary marketing spend
Review promotional eligibility
Monitor bonus dependence
Focus on improving customer economics

The proposed framework recommends measuring campaign performance using metrics such as CLV uplift, retention rate and ROI by customer segment.

# Key Business Insights

The analysis generated several commercially relevant insights:

1. Product profitability matters
Racing and sports customers can have materially different profitability profiles.

2. High turnover does not necessarily mean high CLV
Large bettors can create substantially greater variability in bookmaker returns.

3. Bonus behaviour is an important profitability signal
Bonus-heavy customers can generate negative economics and should be considered separately in CLV modelling.

4. Customer behaviour during the first 20 bets can provide valuable predictive signals
Early betting frequency, stake size, product mix, profitability and promotional behaviour can be used to estimate future customer value.

5. CLV should support decisions, not just predictions
The ultimate objective is to connect predictions with marketing investment, retention, promotions and customer strategy.
